Why this path can be worth it

More than one kind of freedom.

01

Strong earning range

Cybersecurity can grow from solid early-career pay into high-value specialist work. Cleared IT professionals in their first two years averaged about $82,753 in a 2026 industry report.

02

Work with meaning

Protect people, data, public services, and systems that matter. The work can range from a local hospital to national-security missions.

03

Flexible lanes

Commercial cloud and security roles can be remote or hybrid. Classified work often happens onsite, but may offer unusual missions, stability, and access to scarce experience.

04

A future-facing specialty

AI systems still need identity, permissions, monitoring, secure infrastructure, and human judgment. Cyber foundations transfer directly into the emerging AI-security market.

Choose your portal

Two strong paths. Different kinds of freedom.

The beginning is nearly identical: foundations, Security+, Linux, cloud, labs, and communication. The environment changes after that. Neither lane is automatically better; the right one fits her priorities.

G

Portal A · Mission lane

Government + cleared work

Best for someone drawn to mission impact, structured environments, public service, and technical experience that can be difficult to access elsewhere.

Power-ups

  • Clearance can create a smaller, more defensible talent pool.
  • Work may involve consequential national-security or public-sector missions.
  • Some employers offer strong stability, training, and long-term programs.
  • Service-backed scholarships may reduce education costs.

Tradeoffs

  • Classified work is commonly onsite or hybrid in secure facilities.
  • The hiring and clearance process can take time and is never guaranteed.
  • Location options may cluster around government and defense hubs.
  • Public portfolios can be harder because sensitive work stays private.
Security+→Sponsor role→Clearance→Mission AI security

Great fit if she says: “I can trade some flexibility for meaningful work, rare access, and a durable specialty.”

C

Portal B · Builder lane

Commercial + cloud work

Best for someone who values mobility, public proof, faster experimentation, and the ability to move across industries, products, and company sizes.

Power-ups

  • Remote and hybrid options are generally more available.
  • Public cloud and open-source projects make portfolio building easier.
  • Skills can transfer across healthcare, finance, retail, startups, and tech.
  • Fast-moving teams can offer quicker exposure to new AI tooling.

Tradeoffs

  • Entry-level applicant pools can be larger and more competitive.
  • Startup and product roles may have less stability or clearer boundaries.
  • Remote roles attract national or global competition.
  • Technology cycles move quickly, so continued learning is mandatory.
Security+→Cloud support→Cloud security→Product AI security

Great fit if she says: “I want flexibility, visible projects, and the freedom to move between industries.”

The secret third option: move between them.

Commercial cloud experience can strengthen a later cleared application, and mission experience can become valuable in regulated commercial industries. The shared foundation keeps both doors open.

        The quest map

        Six checkpoints. One credible story.

        Move in order, but do not wait for perfection. Each checkpoint should create a small piece of evidence that proves the skill is becoming real.

        1. 1

          Trailhead · Weeks 1–4

          Learn the language of computers

          Build a working mental model of hardware, operating systems, networking, users, permissions, files, and the command line.

          • Use CompTIA A+ material as the map; test only if it helps her confidence or job search.
          • Practice Linux commands and basic networking every week.
          • Create a GitHub profile and write short lab notes in plain English.
          Proof: home lab map + troubleshooting journal
        2. 2

          First badge · Months 2–4

          Earn Security+

          Security+ gives her an industry-recognized baseline in threats, architecture, operations, identity, risk, and incident response.

          • Study in short visual blocks: concept map, lab, explain-it-back, practice questions.
          • Turn every exam domain into one diagram or mini-lab.
          • Begin applying before the exam is complete when listings say “ability to obtain.”
          Proof: Security+ + visual study portfolio
        3. 3

          The sponsor gate · Months 3–8

          Target the job that can sponsor clearance

          A person cannot apply for a U.S. security clearance alone. An eligible employer or government organization must initiate the process for a role that requires it.

          • Search: help desk, junior SOC, security analyst, systems administrator, cloud support.
          • Look for “ability to obtain,” “clearance eligible,” or sponsorship language.
          • Keep finances, records, and application facts organized and truthful.
          Proof: targeted resume + 20-role tracker
        4. 4

          Systems power-up · Months 5–10

          Become dangerous with Linux, Python, and AWS

          Move beyond memorizing security terms. Learn how production systems are actually built, automated, observed, and repaired.

          • RHCSA-style Linux administration: users, services, storage, logs, networking.
          • Python for small automations, parsing logs, and calling APIs.
          • AWS fundamentals: IAM, VPC, EC2, S3, CloudTrail, GuardDuty, and budgets.
          Proof: hardened cloud lab + incident walkthrough
        5. 5

          Future-proof zone · Months 8–14

          Add AI security to the foundation

          Learn the new attack surface without abandoning classic security: data poisoning, prompt injection, excessive agency, model access, supply-chain risk, and evaluation.

          • Use NIST AI RMF for risk thinking and MITRE ATLAS for adversary behavior.
          • Study the OWASP risks for generative and agentic applications.
          • Build systems with least privilege, logs, approval gates, and clear failure modes.
          Proof: secure AI project + threat model
        6. 6

          Boss level · Year 1–3

          Choose the scarce intersection

          Combine hands-on infrastructure, cybersecurity, AI risk, and mission context. The goal is not “knowing AI.” It is being trusted to secure systems that use it.

          • Target AI security engineering, secure MLOps, AI assurance, cloud AI security, or AI red teaming.
          • Keep a portfolio that shows decisions, controls, evidence, and business impact.
          • Build relationships in cleared-tech, public-sector cloud, and AI-security communities.
          Target: cleared AI security infrastructure engineer
